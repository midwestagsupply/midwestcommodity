# assets/

## The company marks

`logo-badgergrain.png`        1200 x 400 px, transparent background
`logo-midwestcommodity.png`    832 x 400 px, transparent background

**Both files are in this folder as of 2026-09-18.** The numbers above are
measured off the delivered files, not requested. Midwest's is 832 wide and not
600: at 600x400 `object-fit:contain` drew the mark 38.7% smaller than the
masthead box allows, because the canvas was padded rather than the mark small.
Anything replacing it should be its own aspect, not padded to a round number.

**They were rebuilt, not supplied.** The only reference was a 205x98 crop of a
screenshot of an email. The leaf is traced from those pixels as Bezier curves;
the lettering is the closest bundled face fitted to the crop by least squares
and converted to outlines, then the sublines eroded to match the original's
stroke weight. Good enough for the sites. NOT good enough for a sign, a truck
door or anything printed large -- the letterforms are a near miss, not the
mark. If Kristi ever sends the originals, swapping them in is a file copy.

Each site uses BOTH files. Its own mark is the masthead; the other company's
mark is the "Our other location" card at the foot of the page, so the two
repositories carry the same two files.

WHAT THEY SHOULD BE.
  Midwest Commodity Service Inc. -- a green leaf above "MIDWEST" in heavy
  black type, with "COMMODITY SERVICE INC." letterspaced beneath.
  Badger Grain Supply LLC -- "BADGER GRAIN" in heavy maroon with a small
  maroon leaf to the upper right, and "SUPPLY LLC" in light grey beneath.

SIZES ARE A FLOOR, NOT A SHAPE. The stylesheet fits the artwork into a fixed
box with `object-fit:contain`, so any proportion works and nothing is cropped
or stretched, and the page reserves the same space whatever arrives. What
matters is resolution: the masthead box is 430 x 118 CSS px at its largest, so
anything under about 900 x 250 real pixels will look soft on a phone. The two
sizes above are comfortably over 2x and still over 3x.

UNTIL THEY ARRIVE, the pages are not broken. Each `<img>` carries the company
name as its `alt` text, styled in the site's own display face and its own brand
colour, so a visitor sees the name set as lettering rather than a broken-image
icon. See "THE COMPANIES' OWN LOGOS" at the foot of `site.css`.

THERE IS NO REVERSED VARIANT AND NOTHING NEEDS ONE. The dark rendering was
removed on 2026-08-31 at the owner's request and `:root{color-scheme:light}` is
what is left of it; both grounds these marks sit on are `--paper`, white. If a
dark ground ever returns, ask for `logo-badgergrain-reversed.png` and
`logo-midwestcommodity-reversed.png` at the same pixel sizes, with the maroon
and the green lightened enough to hold 4.5:1 on the dark paper and the black
and grey lettering set in paper white. Do not draw them before there is
somewhere to put them.

## The corn, which stays

`corn-left.png` / `corn-right.png`
  For light backgrounds. Black engraving, gold kernels.

`corn-left-reversed.png` / `corn-right-reversed.png`
  For dark backgrounds. White engraving, gold kernels.

Both pairs are now UNUSED BY THE PAGE. They drew the old masthead lockup either
side of a wordmark set in type (`.wm1` / `.wmrule` / `.wm2` in `site.css`),
and on 2026-09-18 that lockup was replaced by the company marks above -- "keep
our old logo's so people recognize it better even though we do really like your
corns!". The files and their CSS are kept so that going back is one markup edit.

The reversed pair has never been drawn on either site: see the note about the
dark rendering above.

`icon.png`, `icon-192.png`, `icon-512.png`
  The app icons and the favicon, and STILL CORN, deliberately. The browser tab,
  the home-screen icon and the link-preview card (`og.png`, generated by
  `tools/make-og.mjs`) were all left alone when the marks changed. Do not
  "tidy" them to match the masthead without asking.

Before any of this goes on a sign, a truck or an invoice, have the mark
redrawn as vector. A 1200px PNG is a web file.
